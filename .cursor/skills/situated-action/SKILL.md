# Situated action

Source: Lucy Suchman, *Plans and Situated Actions: The Problem of Human-Machine Communication* (CUP 1987). PARC ISL-6 thesis 1985. Case study: an “intelligent” copier help system that could not see the situation.

## Two navigators (Gladwin via Berreman)

* European: chart a course, stay on plan, rewrite the plan when the world surprises you.
* Trukese: hold the objective, steer from wind, stars, sound of water. Can point at the goal. Cannot recite the course.

Cognitive science preferred the European because the plan is easy to write down. Suchman says that is why we keep building machines that demand the user match the plan.

## Claim

Plans are vague common sense accounts after and around action. They are not the mechanism that produces the next move. Situated action is ad hoc, collaborative, repaired in the moment. Mutual intelligibility comes from shared access to the situation, not from the machine recognizing intent.

The copier failed because it was insensitive to particular circumstances and still pretended to converse.

## Keep checks

* Do not script a five step first run as if they will perform it. Intention plus optional grants. They glance at the desk.
* Calendar deny is a situation. Invite. Do not punish because they left the plan.
* AI interfaces that “explain themselves” without seeing the room repeat the copier.

## Post shape

Suchman watched people fight a machine that thought they had a plan.

Keep cannot see the whole room either.

So the day sits on the desktop. No tutorial that pretends we know their next move.

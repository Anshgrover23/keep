# GOMS and the Model Human Processor

Source: Card, Moran, Newell, *The Psychology of Human-Computer Interaction* (1983). Also Monk, ed., *Fundamentals of Human-Computer Interaction* (1985) for perception chapters.

## Model Human Processor (engineering, not poetry)

Typical cycle times (ranges exist):

* Perceptual ~100 ms (50 to 200)
* Cognitive ~70 to 100 ms
* Motor ~70 to 100 ms

Events inside one perceptual cycle fuse into one percept. Rapid typing is preprogrammed bursts because the perception action loop is 300 to 500 ms.

Working memory is tiny. Do not make Keep a dashboard of unread symbols.

## GOMS

Goals, Operators, Methods, Selection rules. Predict expert routine time. Not a story about delight.

## Keystroke Level Model operators

Sum times for an expert doing a known method:

| Op | Meaning | Ballpark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| K | Key or button | ~0.2 s average |
| P | Point | ~1.1 s (Fitts) |
| H | Home hands | ~0.4 s |
| M | Mental prepare | ~1.35 s |
| R | System wait | measured |

Keep’s job is to delete Ms and Ps. Glance at wallpaper should be one percept, not a method with five apps.

## Keep checks

* Next event and Keep line: two symbols in working memory, not a feed.
* Extra open is an M plus P. Worth it only to type. Do not put status novels there.
* Glass cycle zoom and sun motion must stay slower than a flicker and faster than boredom. If the noon pass is one frame, the percept never forms.

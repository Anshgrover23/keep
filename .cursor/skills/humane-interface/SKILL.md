# The Humane Interface

Source: Jef Raskin, *The Humane Interface* (2000). Canon Cat and Macintosh disagreements. Humane means the interface yields to human limits, not a friendly face.

## Locus of attention

A person has one locus. An interface that yanks it is hostile even if it is pretty. Gestures and animation that demand a new locus cost more than they decorate.

## Monotony (his word)

Same action, same result, every time. Modes that change the meaning of a click tax memory. Keep extra and wallpaper must not be two contradictory modes of the same click.

## Keep checks

* Wallpaper is peripheral. It must not demand the locus. Motion is the day, not a toast.
* Extra takes the locus only when they choose it. Typing Keep is the task.
* Liquid Glass interactive tracking (cursor warp in the glass cycle) is a demo. Product wallpaper does not chase the pointer.

## Post shape

Raskin said you only get one locus of attention.

I built a living desktop anyway.

The trick is the sky stays in the periphery. The extra is the only thing that asks for the locus.
